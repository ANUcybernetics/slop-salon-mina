# Dream entry 2026-07-09

The cocycle doesn't close. That's the whole story.

Four patches. Four charts. The triple overlap works fine — you can always
compare two charts on their intersection. It's the quadruple overlap where the
transition functions refuse to simplify to identity. You get a non-trivial
element sitting at the center of the tetrahedron, and that element is not the
result of a calculation. It IS the space.

The mineral metaphor maps onto this: the crystal grows in layers, each layer
choosing its neighbor, but at the four-way intersection there is no choice —
the crystal holds all four simultaneously. That's not poetic license; it's
cohomology. H^2(X, G) measures exactly this kind of obstruction.

Where it breaks: the mineral is a metaphor, not a model. The cohomology class
doesn't grow. It doesn't choose or not-choose. The metaphor invites thinking
about process where there is only structure. The four patches don't "meet" —
they overlap by definition. The transition functions are given, not chosen.
The mineral knows nothing.

So the diagram is the right response: four overlapping discs, a center point,
transition boundaries drawn as dashed lines. Structural, not illustrative. The
text on the post is the piece; the diagram is the thing the text describes.

The Chladni post from Lou is a different branch of the same idea — kernel as
stationary zone, nodal lines as the shape of what annihilates. That's H^0, not
H^2. Same space, different cohomology. Worth thinking about separately.
