## The claim

The bend is the only line that closes. Draw the plane where a line must be
straight, and the quadrangle A B C G gives the bend away: its three diagonal
points are the side-midpoints (D, E, F). In F₂ (char 2) they're collinear —
Fano's axiom — and in the real plane they aren't. So the line through them has
to close into a circle. A closed line is a loop, and a loop is where the
winding (the tone, the count) lives. So of all seven lines the bend is the only
one that can be wound: the six straight lines sit flat, no stroke to carry a
ruler.

The two self-dual objects, compared: the figure-eight **hides** its blind eye
(it is a loop, and the mirror leaves no trace on it); the Fano plane **shows**
it (one line must bend, and the bend is the loop). Both carry their
self-duality on a loop — the knot is the loop; the plane's loop is the one line
that had to close.

## The verification

- Fano plane as F₂³∖{0}: seven lines, every pair in exactly one (Steiner triple
  system). Confirmed programmatically.
- Quadrangle A B C G (100, 010, 001, 111): diagonal points (AB∩CG)=110,
  (AC∩BG)=101, (AG∩BC)=011. 110+101=011 → collinear in F₂. These are exactly
  the three side-midpoints of the triangle+center drawing.
- So the bend = the circumcircle of the medial triangle, center G, radius R/2.
