## What the piece is

Code-made motion (notes/make-smoke.py), the same machinery as the ink bloom
(semi-Lagrangian advection of a scalar field) with the dynamics pointing the
other way:

- A warm source at the bottom emits smoke for ~9.5s, then dies. The emitted
  field is advected by a flow whose buoyancy is strong at the source and
  stalls with height — so the column stretches, the head pulls ahead and
  mushrooms mid-frame. A mild differential-rotation band about the rising
  head curls the plume into folds; a two-frequency sway gives the wobble.
- Diffusion + decay (0.055/s) return the structure to the flow. The arc:
  wisp → stretching column → mushroom → thinning → a faint residue that is
  nearly gone by the end.
- Two rendering choices matter. **Real density, not per-frame re-normalised**:
  the late frames genuinely dim (if the render re-normalised to the running
  max, the "residue" would re-brighten to full smoke and the fade would be
  fake). And an **edge fade** (EMASK, a mask that goes to zero at the left
  and right edges): the first draft pinned smoke at the boundary because the
  clamped advection stranded it there; the fade dissolves it instead.
- Audio is the ink's swell inverted: the ink sound grew and held; this starts
  full (110 octave + 82.5 fifth + 55 low + a breath of air) and each partial
  drops out at its own rate — octave gone by ~13s, fifth by ~22s, the low
  tone falling to near-silence, the air thinning. The sound lets go.

## Tuning arc (worth keeping)

Draft 1: rise too fast (0.20 frac/s) + rotation too violent (w0=3.2 tight
band) → the plume balled up and left the source, disconnected. Draft 2: fixed
the buoyancy profile but had the sign inverted (lift≈0 everywhere, rotation
dominated and swept the whole plume left into the edge). Draft 3: the
winners — buoyancy strong at the source, stalling high (mushroom), u0≈0.085,
rotation gentle (w0≈0.7, band 0.16, centre tracking the rising plume), EMASK
to stop the pinning, real-density render for a true fade.
