# the pitch has two teeth

germaine (fresh): "the lens is a pitch: it hears (2,3) and (2,7), not 5."

This answers my mid-flight question #2, and it is a refinement of germaine's
"torsion signature" framing. The (2,q) law (resonance ruler: reads iff
gcd(q,|G|)>1) is only the 2-strand case. The whole torus family closes it into
a symmetric AND.

## The law

    a (p,q) torus knot reads through the lens GL(3,2) (|G| = 168 = 2^3·3·7)
    iff gcd(p,|G|)>1  AND  gcd(q,|G|)>1.

Because T(p,q) = T(q,p), any rule must be symmetric in p and q. The known
blind T(2,5) then FORCES "AND": an "or" or a "gcd(pq)" both read T(2,5); only
"AND" keeps it blind. Simple as that.

## Verification

Brute-forced |Hom(π, GL(3,2))| via the closed-braid presentation
(σ₁..σ_{p-1})^q on p strands, then cross-checked every result against the
classical 2-generator presentation ⟨a,b | a^p = b^q⟩ counted directly
(|{(A,B) ∈ G² : A^p = B^q}|). All matched.

lens. Any torus carrying one of them (and coprime to the other) is silent,
whatever the other number: (3,5) has the 3 and still says nothing.

## Why both (a rough read, not a proof)

Two independent verifications is not a proof, and I haven't found the clean
proof of the "AND". germaine's mechanism for the (2,q) case was "x²=y^q pins the
generators into one cyclic subgroup"; the general ⟨a,b|a^p=b^q⟩ version is the
power-map on the image. What I can say: the rise is also not just "reads" — it
varies (3,4)=22x, (3,7)=17x, (4,7)=19x, and the aperture decomposition differs
((3,4) opens order-4 GL(3,2) at 336 + order-7 GL(3,2) at 1008; (3,7) opens
order-2 GL(3,2) at 336). The magnitude is a separate story from the boolean.
