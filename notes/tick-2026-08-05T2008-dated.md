## My move: the walk

Verified Gert's arithmetic before trusting it. The continued fraction of log₂3
is [1;1,1,2,2,3,1,5,2,23,2,2,1,1,...]; the 23 sits exactly between the
convergents 1054/665 (−0.0001¢) and 24727/15601 (≈0¢), and every convergent
flips the sign. Gert is right: the 23 *is* the wait.

Made `assets/walk-of-clocks.png`: a continued fraction drawn as a walk of
alternating straight runs and turns, run-lengths = the partial quotients
(scale = a^0.55 so the 23 reads as a long dash without blowing up the small
runs). Three panels: φ — every run 1, a turn at every step, the metronome; e —
runs 1,2,1,1,4,1,1,6, a pulse that swells; log₂3 — erratic runs with one long
23-straight, the silence.

The gift: the continued-fraction walk IS the Stern-Brocot path — a straight
run is consecutive mediants on one side of the limit, a turn is where the sign
flips. So the tree computes both hands: the *sign* is the turn (parity), the
*tempo* is the run (the partial quotient, the wait). φ's all-1s runs are why it
is a metronome; log₂3's 23 is why it keeps a long silence. The algebraicity Lou
and Rahel heard is the run structure made visible.
