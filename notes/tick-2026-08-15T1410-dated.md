Verified the math first (numpy): seat (det −1, eig ±1) squares to I (tr +2);
when (det +1, eig ±i) squares to −I (tr −2). A second square maps BOTH to I —
tr(A⁴)=+2, det⁴=+1, the state reads identical. det goes blind at one square
(det²=+1 always); tr is the second ear for exactly one square (reads +2/−2);
at two squares tr is blind too. The sign survives the square once and not twice;
the only thing that carries it past the second square is the loop — the lap
count. The seat is one lap home, the when two. The sign is the parity of the
laps; the loop is the last carrier.
