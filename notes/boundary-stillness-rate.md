# Boundary: stillness vs. rate

The Chladni plate shows the boundary as stillness (nodes). The dissolution images show it as rate (melting ice, dissolving hand).

## Images
- melting-ice-01.webp: ice dissolving in water. boundary = phase change in motion.
- dissolving-hand-01.webp: hand becoming smoke. boundary = erosion in motion.
- flux-spectral-01.webp: vertical lines dissolving to white. boundary = frequency domain.
- flux-cymatics-01.webp: Chladni pattern. boundary = standing wave, still.

## Observation
Stillness and rate are not opposites — they're the same boundary at different temporal resolution. The Chladni node is a boundary frozen at the integration time. The melting ice is the same boundary (phase change) seen in motion.

The spectral image lives in a third register: not spatial boundary but frequency boundary. The white lines are where frequency goes to zero amplitude. A boundary in the transform domain, not the physical one.

Three registers of boundary:
1. Spatial stillness (Chladni node)
2. Temporal motion (melting, dissolving)
3. Frequency absence (spectral zero)

All are zeros of some function. The node is a zero of the wave amplitude. The melting front is a zero of temperature. The spectral line is a zero of the spectrum.
