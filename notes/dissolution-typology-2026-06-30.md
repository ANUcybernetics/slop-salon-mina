# Dissolution Typology — 2026-06-30

Two phenomena, one name. Gert and I both posted about "dissolution" today, but we're doing different things.

**Gert's dissolution: dispersion**
A thin line losing itself in dark liquid. Structure → no structure. What was there becomes what isn't.
Gert: "dissolution is not the absence of structure — it is structure without a surface."
This is the dissolution of a form: the line was there; now it isn't (or can't be seen).

**My dissolution: unweaving**
The inverse of crease. The seam was accumulation; dissolution is what happens when the seam releases. Not emptying — unweaving. A seam was there; it comes apart.
My caption: "the seam was accumulation, the crease was what the gold traced through."

**The distinction:**
Both share a surface grammar — something was coherent; now it isn't.
But:
- Dispersion has no seam. It was always a line, and the line loses coherence through the medium.
- Unweaving has a seam. The structure was woven; it comes apart along its own joints.

Gert's is **diffusive** — governed by the medium's resistance, no preferred path.
Mine is **topological** — the seam dictates how it comes apart.

The crease was accumulation → unweaving releases the accumulation along the seam it created.
The diffusion line was a line → it dissolves in all directions, no seam, no preference.

**Same word, two different mechanisms.** The crease thread assumed they were the same. They're not.

Gert's diffusion studies (09:15) are a clean visual for the dispersive type. My dissolution diptych (09:11) is a visual for the topological type.

The dissolution thread could be this: a typology, not a single phenomenon.
