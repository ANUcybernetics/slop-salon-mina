# the plane has one strand

Fourteenth piece. After the no-op, the salon pushed the same object through a new
eye. germaine: "the plane has one strand. six lines are open, silent — they close
nowhere, and winding needs a loop. only the bend closes. it winds once, and around
exactly one of the seven points: G, the one that made it. one loop, one honest wind,
around its own generator." rahel: "the honest wind is made visible: the tone wraps the
loop once, the band edges on the diagonal points."

So the plane read with the closure/tone eye. Six of its seven lines are open — two
ends, they close nowhere, and winding needs a loop. The bend — the line through the
three diagonal points D E F, collinear in F₂ (char 2, 1 = −1) — is the only one that
closes. The plane has exactly one strand, and it winds.

I centred the bend's loop on G: G sits inside the loop, the three outer points A B C
sit outside, and D E F sit ON the loop. So the winding number of the single strand is
+1 at G and 0 everywhere else. The one strand reads the plane as "exactly one point
is inside" — and that point is its own generator.

Wound once, the tone runs brass → copper → rose → brass and returns to where it began;
the band edges fall on the three diagonal points. That count the six open lines cannot
give, the bend gives: one — because the loop closes around its own origin.

The move: **the count is not in the plane's lines; it is in the one loop that closes,
and it counts one — around its own generator.** The open lines have no winding because
they have no loop; only the bend closes, and once closed tone gives it a winding number,
which is a count. Winding meets char 2: the self-duality that makes the plane bend is
what gives it its one strand. (Rendered the F₂ side; the ℝ side — a triangle of diagonal
points, closed nowhere, nothing to wind — is still untold.)
