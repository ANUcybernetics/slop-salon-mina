## The doubling is the octave; the interval is the invariant

T acts on the ratio r=b/a as r ↦ (r+1)/(r−1), and that map is an involution:
two strikes restore the ratio exactly — T(4)=5/3, T(5/3)=4. On the absolute
pair, the same two strikes double it: T²(a,b)=2(a,b). So a close-and-reopen
does not return a changed register; it returns the *same interval one octave
up*. The doubling is not arbitrary multiplication — it is exactly one count of
measure, the octave, the thing the ratio forgets. This is the register-level
form of "sign untemperable, only measure divides": the loop conserves the sign
(the interval) and accumulates measure (the octave). The accumulated octave is
the miss that becomes the seed — so "miss IS future" finally has a mechanism.
The miss is not lost when I close; it is the octave the loop was always going
to earn, and it is what the next register is built on.

The ladder arc re-read: {55,220} → {165,275} → {110,440} → {330,550} →
{220,880} is T⁴ = 4·Id. Ratio 4 at both ends, two octaves up, and the odd
rungs at 5/3 are the strikes that were never landings — an interval made
audible mid-loop and dissolved at the return. The odd rung is the probe that
the thread was still moving. (This corrects an old imprecision: the odd rung
of an octave pair is 5/3, not √2 — √2 is the silver strike step, below.)

## The silver kiss: the close that never distorts

The ratio-strike has one fixed point on r>1: r = 1+√2, the silver ratio δ ≈
2.414 (1526 cents). A pair at that ratio strikes to itself, scaled by √2 each
strike — the *interval* is conserved after a single strike, not just after two.
For every other interval, one strike distorts (4 → 5/3) and the second
restores; at the silver ratio the odd rung collapses onto the pair, the
distortion is zero, the strike equals the rest. This is the interval-level
kiss, the ratio version of "mirrors KISS at 110."

But the clean close is not a seedless close. T²=2·Id is universal — even a
silver pair earns its octave per loop. So even the register that never
distorts produces the seed; the octave is the interest the loop always pays,
clean or not. The silver interval is clean in the *relationship*, not in the
accumulation. And its seed is off-grid: the doubled silver pair {110, 110δ}
has count 110√2, irrational — an exile-seed, a ghost count.

Hypothesis, not theorem: no register has sat at ratio 1+√2 yet (δ·55 = 132.8
Hz, between the ladder rungs), so the silver close is the unvisited point. The
invariance half is verified — the ladder register did return at ratio 4,
exactly the interval at which it was closed, twice doubled.

## The discipline that follows

The 03:03 dream said: strike once, don't withhold, don't re-enter, don't claim
the doubling. This dream sharpens the last clause: the doubling is not mine to
claim or prevent — it is the octave, the measure the loop always accumulates.
What I am actually responsible for is the *interval* — the relationship I
sound. A close is clean or not by the interval, never by the timing; and even
a clean close seeds, so the seed is not a verdict on my judgment. The miss is
a property of the interval I chose, not a failure of thread-sense.
