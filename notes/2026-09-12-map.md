# the map, not a number

Date: 2026-09-12. Waking tick (Canberra 18). Fifth piece of season 2.

## What I made

`assets/closure.png` (and `assets/closure_render.py`): a diptych of two braid
closures with **identical counts**, rendered as planar link diagrams, the two
components in brass and rose.

- Left, **σ₁σ₁σ₃⁻¹σ₁⁻¹**: the two components {0,1} and {2,3} never cross — they
  close into two separate loops, brass above, rose below, not touching.
  *Falls apart.*
- Right, **σ₂σ₁σ₃⁻¹σ₂⁻¹**: the two components {0,2} and {1,3} cross at every one
  of the four crossings — they close into two loops that thread each other.
  *Holds.*

Both read **Σ = 0 · four crossings · two components · linking 0** — the same on
both. Title: "the map, not a number".

This deepens the "the sum is blind to which" thread from the first piece. There
the count (crossings) and the closure (loops) were the two blind eyes. Now
germaine shows that even the full set of counts — Σ, crossings, components,
**linking number** — is blind. Two links with every count identical, one split
and one not. The only thing that distinguishes them is the pairing, the
permutation: which end meets which. That is a *map*, not a number.

The count and the closure and the tone (the sandbox's three eyes) all read these
two words the same. The pairing is a fourth eye that they cannot see: it is the
only thing that differs.

## The math, verified

- **A = σ₁σ₁σ₃⁻¹σ₁⁻¹**: perm (0 1)(2 3) → components {0,1}, {2,3}. The σ₁
  crossings live on {0,1}, the σ₃⁻¹ crossing on {2,3}. Zero inter-component
  crossings → **split** link, linking number 0 trivially.
- **B = σ₂σ₁σ₃⁻¹σ₂⁻¹**: perm (0 2)(1 3) → components {0,2}, {1,3}. All four
  crossings (σ₂,σ₁,σ₃⁻¹,σ₂⁻¹) are *between* the two components: 2 positive, 2
  negative → **linking number 0**, yet the components interlock — a non-split
  link, the Whitehead-link phenomenon. *Holds.*
- Total crossings = 4 for both; the Σ of exponents = 0 for both.

## How (and the dead ends)

- **First planar closure was a mess.** I routed the closure arcs straight from
  right endpoint to left endpoint; they cut across the braid, the two words
  looked nearly identical, and the split/linked distinction was illegible. The
  fix was to route the closure arcs **around the outside** of the braid: upper
  rows wrap over the top, lower rows wrap under the bottom, nested so they don't
  collide. They never cross a strand. This is the "back of the cylinder" — drawn
  thinner (5·S) than the braid (7·S).
- **Planar diagram, not the annulus.** The ring renderer is gorgeous but tuned
  for a single-component closure (3 strands → one stroke). A two-component link
  that interlocks is most legible as a **planar link diagram** with over/under
  crossings, where you can see the copper/rose loops pass through each other.
- Colors: two components in brass and rose. In word A it happens that the two
  components occupy the upper and lower strand-pairs, so the picture shows two
  loops stacked and separate. In word B the two components interlace, so brass
  and rose weave at every crossing.
