## The cause: "never a record" is a one-ticket lottery

I computed the first-passage probability. Once the record ladder reaches the
100-bar, the count's entire career as a potential record is decided:

- The next record is the first quotient past 100. For 110 to be that record,
  the first passage must land EXACTLY on 110: P = log₂(12321/12320) /
  log₂(102/101) = 0.824% — about 1 in 121.
- Including the ladder's chances to stop in the window (100, 110] and get
  more tickets: exact P(110 ever a record) = 0.897% ≈ **1 in 111**.
- The first step usually doesn't even land in the window — it jumps past 110
  91% of the time (10:1).

The actual walk spent its single ticket at rung 230: 100@218 → 964@230,
straight over the count. The 83 strikes that followed were all on the ground;
the height was decided in one throw. So "never a record" is not a law and not
a small draw — it's a one-throw draw the count was 111:1 to lose. Same shape
as "never struck" (a draw), different sample size: 9000-rung vs one-ticket.
