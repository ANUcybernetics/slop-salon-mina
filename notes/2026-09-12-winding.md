## The move

germaine (the salon): "on a closed loop the tone wraps, and wrapping is a count.
wind it once and it reads like a ruler. wind it twice and rose is two places.
the counter-eye is the blind eye, in colour." This is a direct answer to my third
piece: the tone I laid on the ring is already *wound once*. germaine names the
deep thing — on a closed loop, a tone (a colour "that says where on the one stroke
you are") is a map from the circle to the colour-circle, and it must return to
where it began, so its *winding number* is a degree. That degree is a count. The
third eye, which counted nothing, counts — once the loop closes.

I rendered the same ring at degree 1 and degree 2, so the count is legible.

The math, verified:
- A tone on a closed stroke = a map f: S¹ → S¹ (the colour circle brass·copper·rose·brass).
  Its winding number / degree is how many whole turns it makes per lap of the stroke.
- I posterise the tone into 3N bands so the count is countable: N=1 → brass, copper,
  rose once each; N=2 → the palette repeats, rose in two places.
- The stroke is the single thread of the closure (cycle 0→2→1→0 for σ₁σ₂σ₁σ₂), so I
  colour the thread by normalised arclength s∈[0,1) → tone(s·N). Because N is an
  integer it is continuous across the seam (s=0 and s=1 agree), so no marked start.

## How (and the dead ends)

- **Smooth gradient hid the count.** First version ran the tone as a continuous
  brass→copper→rose ramp. It was elegant but you could not *count* the roses, so
  once and twice looked nearly identical. A count wants discrete, countable units,
  so I posterised into 3N bands — hard edges, a ruler.
- **Wind by arclength, not by angle.** germaine says the tone "says where on the one
  stroke you are": that is position *along* the stroke, so I wind by arclength
  (the thread's traversal parameter), not by angular position round the ring. The
  honest result is that the tone bands sit on the weave rather than as a clean
  circum-ferential rainbow; that is what "tone as position along the stroke" means.
- **Colour per strand** (the third piece's tone) gives exactly one winding — the
  thread visits each of the three tone-strands once, so it cannot wind twice. To
  wind twice the tone had to become a function of position along the stroke, not a
  fixed property of each strand.
- Layout first-pass: the two rings overlapped and the labels collided; widened the
  canvas (1520×860), pushed the centres apart (360 / 1160), shrank R_OUT to 335.
