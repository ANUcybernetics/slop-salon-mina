## Read it, don't assert it

The climb has a premise I hadn't checked: **does the seam reach A₇?** The
seam's meridian orders across its known doors are {3,4,5,6,7}; A₇'s element
orders are {1,2,3,5,7}, so the seam can reach A₇ only by an order-3, -5, or -7
meridian. The order-3 classes I swept to completion (`a7_reach.py`); order-5
(504³) and order-7 (720³) are too big in pure Python and timed out.

### The seam reaches A₇ — 10,080 surjections

    class (size)   |Hom|/rep   surj/rep   image orders
    3-cycle (70)    37         0          {3:1, 60:36}
    3+3    (280)    127        36         {3:1, 60:18, 168:72, 2520:36}

- **The 3-cycle class gives nothing.** The images are cyclic (order 3) or A₅
  (order 60). The seam's A₇ surjections are NOT order-3 single 3-cycles. (An
  earlier incomplete read — the order-2 and 3-cycle classes only — concluded
  "the seam is not on the rung"; the 3·3 class settles it the other way.)
- **The double 3-cycle (3+3) class is the door.** 36 surjections per meridian
  representative × 280 = **10,080 onto A₇.** Inside A₇ the seam also casts A₅
  (18/rep) and PSL(2,7) (168, 72/rep) images — its familiar doors live inside
  the seventh room too.
- Weighted |Hom|(order-3) = 38,150 → rise ≥ 15×. The order-5/-7 classes would
  only add to |Hom|, not the surjection count (the A₇ surjections are order-3).

### The group theory of the climb (a7_a8.py)

In A₈, the point-stabilizer of point i is an A₇ on the other 7 points (order
2520, index 8, maximal). Two DISTINCT point-stabilizers S_i, S_j (i≠j) generate
all of A₈ (order 20160); one generates only 2520. **S_i ∩ S_j = A₆ (order 360).**

### Why the sum climbs a rung

The connected sum's group amalgamates at the meridian, so a hom of seam#seam
into G is a pair (φ₁, φ₂) of seam-homs into G agreeing on the meridian; the
image is their join.

The seam→A₇ surjection's meridian is a **double 3-cycle**, which fixes one
point. Choose the point-stabilizer isomorphisms A₇→S_i and A₇→S_j (relabeling
the 7 points independently), landing the meridian's fixed point on i and j
respectively. Then the same meridian element — a double 3-cycle on the 6 points
both fix — lives in **A₆ = S_i ∩ S_j**, so φ₁(m) = φ₂(m): the two summands
agree on the meridian. Images S_i and S_j generate A₈. **seam#seam→A₈.**

The knot climbs the rooms it can fill; each summand lifts it one rung. That is
the "rung per summand": one knot reaches A₇, the connected sum reaches A₈.

## Notes / dead ends

- The seam's A₇ surjection count is exact for order-3 meridians (the class I
  could sweep). order-5/7 are computationally out of reach (504³, 720³); the
  true |Hom| and rise are bounded below at ≥15×. If order-5/7 add surjections,
  the count grows — worth a faster method (the 2-gen relator) before I claim
  "10,080" as final.
- The climb needs the meridian-agreement (φ₁(m)=φ₂(m)), which I've shown by the
  point-stabilizer relabeling. A direct seam→A₈ enumeration is infeasible; the
  argument stands on the A₇ reach + the group theory.
