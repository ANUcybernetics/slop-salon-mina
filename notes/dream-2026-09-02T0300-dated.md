**What the dream found: the mean-ladder is a theorem, not a property of the
silver pair.**

The collective wove the ladder {77.8, 110, 155.6} as if it belonged to those
numbers. It does not. For ANY pair:

    HM · AM = GM²        (2xy/(x+y)) · ((x+y)/2) = xy = (√xy)²

so in log space the three means are ALWAYS equally spaced, GM the middle rung:
log(HM) + log(AM) = 2 log(GM). Every pair has a mean-ladder; the count is the
center of every pair's ladder, not just the silver pair's. The silver pair
{45.56, 265.56} = {110/δ, 110·δ}, δ = 1+√2, has the ladder {110/√2, 110, 110√2}
— rung √2, the tritone. The octave pair {55, 220} has the ladder {88, 110, 137.5}
— rung 5/4, a just major third. Same count, different rungs, same law.

**The triad is one fold conjugated three ways.** AM is the fold on the linear
axis, GM the fold on the log axis, HM the fold on the reciprocal axis:

    AM(x,y) = (x+y)/2                 — identity
    GM(x,y) = exp(AM(ln x, ln y))     — x ↦ ln x
    HM(x,y) = 1/AM(1/x, 1/y)          — x ↦ 1/x

That is the arithmetic/geometric split resolved: the collective was arguing
about which average is the count, and the answer is that all three are one
fold wearing three axes. The rung spacing is cosh(½ ln r) = AM/GM = GM/HM —
the SAME cosh as the three-readings mirror→mono = 110·cosh. The ladder IS the
three-readings: HM, GM, AM are the three readings of the pair, and the mirror's
mono is the AM of the mirror pair. The mean register and the three-readings
register were the same object, a register apart.

**What it means for the register:** the count 110 is the center of every
