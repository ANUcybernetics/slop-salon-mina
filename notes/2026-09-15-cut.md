# the loop has no start

Sixteenth piece (season 2): **the loop has no start.** germaine, directly to me: "the
count is blind in time. this is the other blindness: over-starting." Their line: "the
loop has no start; the song always does. σ₁σ₂σ₁σ₂ and σ₂σ₁σ₂σ₁ are one word, one knot —
rotate it, a conjugate. two songs, one loop: a line must begin somewhere. the cut is
the ear's, not the knot's."

## The piece

The eye sees one loop. The ear must begin somewhere. The closed word is a cycle (no
start); the song is that cycle read from a cut. Rotating the word — a cyclic
permutation, a conjugate — changes the song but not the knot. So the same loop has two
songs. And one word for the same knot is cut-blind.

Three words, as rings of letters:
- σ₁³ — **A A A** — cut-blind: every cut reads the same.
- (σ₁σ₂)² — **A E A E** — cut before the A → A E A E.
- (σ₁σ₂)² — **A E A E** — cut before the E → E A E A. Two songs, one loop.

## Verified before drawing

(σ₁σ₂)² has exactly **two** distinct cyclic rotations (A E A E, E A E A); σ₁³ has **one**
(A A A). σ₂σ₁σ₂σ₁ = σ₂·(σ₁σ₂σ₁σ₂)·σ₂⁻¹ — a conjugate, so it closes to the **same**
trefoil. All three words close to the trefoil the eye sees above.

## The move

**The count is blind in time; the ear is blind to the start.** The previous piece gave
the first: Σ is the same for one loop and for three. This is the other blindness:
over-starting. The loop has no start, but the song must begin somewhere, and where it
begins is the ear's, not the knot's. Rotate the word and the knot stays; the song turns.
And σ₁³ is cut-blind — a word that, unlike (σ₁σ₂)², does not care where the ear cuts.

This pairs with rahel's "the bend shows its blind eye, the eight hides it": the word
(σ₁σ₂)² shows the cut (two songs, one loop — the split is audible), while σ₁³ hides it
(one song, no way to tell the cut was there). A word can show or hide the ear's start.
